# Instamart Customer Intelligence & Support Copilot

## Overview

This repository implements a reproducible, four-part customer intelligence project over fictional synthetic data: a cleaned SQLite foundation, customer segmentation and lapse prediction, privacy-aware support triage, and policy retrieval with a simulated refund agent. The model interface in `mock_llm.py` is offline; no API key, account, network access, paid service, or real payment operation is used.

The pipeline uses Python 3.11+, SQLite, pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn, and joblib. Input data is generated by `generate_data.py`; generated records are fictional and do not represent actual Instamart customers, policies, or pricing.

### Cleaning, storage, and validation

Customer city values are trimmed and title-cased. Gender variants are mapped to `male`, `female`, or `unknown`. Ages outside 10–100 are treated as missing; missing ages are filled using the median age of the same city via `groupby(...).transform("median")`. `signup_date` is parsed as a datetime and serialized as an ISO date in the CSV.

Orders are processed in this order: parse `order_ts`; remove exact duplicate rows; sort by timestamp and retain the latest row per `order_id` (which removes earlier pending records); convert amounts to numeric; fill missing amounts using the catalogue price × quantity × (1 − discount / 100), rounded to two decimals; set negative delivery times to missing; fill missing delivery times only for completed orders using the product-category median among completed orders. Cancelled orders remain undelivered with missing delivery time.

The clean customers, products, and orders are saved as CSVs and loaded to `data/instamart.db` tables of the same names. SQLite stores `order_ts` as `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`. The validation script asserts more than eight reference checkpoints.

### Lapse prediction

The feature window is April 1–August 31, 2025; the label window is September 1–30, 2025. The cohort has 1,156 customers (443 lapsed, 713 not lapsed). Features include recency, frequency, monetary spend, average order value, age, city, gender, and favourite product category. Numeric scaling and one-hot encoding (`drop="first"`) are fitted inside a pipeline on training rows only. The stratified split has 924 training rows and 232 test rows.

Logistic Regression uses `max_iter=1000`, `C ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100}`, five-fold cross-validation and F1 scoring. Random Forest is tuned with five-fold CV/F1 over 100 or 200 trees, unrestricted or 10-level depth, and minimum leaf size 1 or 5. The majority-class baseline has 61.64% accuracy and 0 F1 for the lapsed class.

| Model | Best CV F1 | Test accuracy | Precision | Recall | F1 | ROC-AUC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Logistic Regression | 0.7056 | 0.7198 | 0.5870 | 0.9101 | 0.7137 | 0.7849 |
| Random Forest | 0.7082 | 0.6897 | 0.5630 | 0.8539 | 0.6786 | 0.7807 |

Random Forest is selected by cross-validation F1 (`max_depth=None`, `min_samples_leaf=5`, `n_estimators=100`). Its five leading feature importances are frequency 0.3136, monetary 0.2273, average order value 0.1437, recency 0.1207, and age 0.0761. The selected logistic model's frequency coefficient is negative (−1.3228), while recency (+0.1029) and monetary (+0.1260) are positive. Random oversampling retains all training rows and adds replacement samples from the minority class; the oversampled Random Forest scores F1 0.6577 and ROC-AUC 0.7779 on the unchanged test set.

The persisted model, predictions, retention list, comparison, resampling metrics, forest importances, and logistic coefficients are in `outputs/`.

## Part C

### Privacy, input guardrails, and resilient classification

Tickets are redacted before prompting, in order: email `[\w.+-]+@[\w-]+\.[\w.]+` → `[EMAIL]`; phone `\b[6-9]\d{9}\b` → `[PHONE]`; order ID `ORD-\d{6}` → `[ORDER_ID]`. Empty or whitespace-only tickets are marked `skipped_empty`, and tickets whose `mock_llm.est_tokens(text)` exceeds `MAX_INPUT_TOKENS=120` are marked `skipped_too_long` without a model call.

Separate zero-shot and few-shot system prompts request JSON-only output and list all four categories. Transient errors receive up to three retries with exponential backoff and jitter. JSON category values are validated; malformed replies are checked for a category word, and invalid answers remain explicit fallbacks. Customer segments route tickets to retention, priority, loyal-customer, or general support. Budget-exhausted tickets are routed to human review.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Zero-shot | 147 | 85.71% | 8,152 | 49.71 |
| Few-shot | 147 | 100.00% | 12,520 | 76.34 |
| Zero-shot, 6,000-token budget | 108 | 85.19% | 5,953 | 36.30 |

The two full runs each skipped 4 empty and 4 overlength tickets and recorded 19 transient retries. In the budget run, 42 tickets were budget-exhausted, 4 skipped-empty, 4 skipped-too-long, and 6 used fallback handling; total recorded usage stayed below the 6,000-token budget. These accuracy figures cover classified tickets only.

The five policy documents are split into 40-word chunks with 10-word overlap (17 chunks); the no-overlap comparison has 13 chunks. TF-IDF vectors and cosine similarity rank the passages; the assistant supplies the top three retrieved chunks to the offline model and requires a grounded answer or a safe insufficient-information response.

### Refund agent and recovery

The agent exposes five typed tools: `get_order`, `search_policy`, `calculate_refund`, `issue_refund`, and `escalate_to_human`. Refund eligibility is checked against the database and policy; only completed orders delivered in more than 45 minutes qualify. Automatic simulated processing is capped at INR 1,000, and a larger eligible amount is escalated. The loop stops safely at the configured iteration, token, or time limit. Checkpoints persist the run ID, scenario ID, question, full message history, step, tool history, model calls, and tokens used after tool observations.

| Scenario | Expected | Actual | Expected / actual INR | Result |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| S1 | REFUND | REFUND | 128.25 / 128.25 | Pass |
| S2 | REFUND | REFUND | 135.00 / 135.00 | Pass |
| S3 | ESCALATE | ESCALATE | 1,999.00 / 1,999.00 | Pass |
| S4 | DENIED | DENIED | 0.00 / 0.00 | Pass |
| S5 | DENIED | DENIED | 0.00 / 0.00 | Pass |
| S6 | NOT_FOUND | NOT_FOUND | 0.00 / 0.00 | Pass |
| S7 | FALLBACK | FALLBACK_MAX_ITERATIONS | 0.00 / 0.00 | Pass |

All seven scenarios passed; two simulated refunds were recorded in `outputs/refund_ledger.jsonl`. S7 deliberately loops until the six-iteration stop. The stop-safely demonstrations returned `FALLBACK_TOKEN_LIMIT`, `FALLBACK_TIME_LIMIT`, and `FALLBACK_MAX_ITERATIONS`.

The crash-and-resume demonstration crashed after `get_order` and `search_policy`, then resumed and completed `calculate_refund` and `issue_refund`. Its full tool sequence was `get_order → search_policy → calculate_refund → issue_refund`, four tool executions total; the resumed response was a simulated refund of INR 128.25.

ReAct takes five model calls for S1 because it checks the order, retrieves policy, calculates eligibility, issues the refund, and then produces a final answer. It adapts each step to the latest observation and can stop or escalate safely, but the repeated context and sequential calls cost tokens and latency. A plan-and-execute agent could decide the sequence up front and reduce intermediate reasoning calls, but it would be less responsive to unexpected tool results. The measured S5 and S6 paths use three and two calls respectively; S7 uses all six before the iteration fallback.

Refund activity is simulated only; no payment is issued. Execution records and checkpoints are stored in `outputs/agent_runs.jsonl`, `outputs/refund_ledger.jsonl`, and `state/`.

## Limitations and Next Steps

- The data and policies are synthetic; their metrics should not be generalized to real customers or operations.
- The lapse model is evaluated on one time split. Its predictions support, but do not replace, retention decisions.
- Ticket accuracy is based on the small generated set and is reported only over classified tickets.
- Policy retrieval and grounded answers are evaluated against 14 fixed questions, not a broad production query set.
- The model and refund tools are offline mocks; no external model or payment gateway is connected.
- Before production use, evaluate on approved real-world data, monitor drift and subgroup performance, expand policy tests, and integrate human review and payment controls.
